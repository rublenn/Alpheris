A

Little's Law: fewer open jobs, faster delivery

Lead time equals work-in-progress divided by throughput. The fastest way to deliver sooner is not to work harder — it's to have fewer things open at once. In knowledge work the penalty for parallelism is worse than linear.

Little's Law / flow metrics [6]

Your estimates are optimistic, predictably

The core correction

One record travelling through eleven stages

A department structure is an org chart, and an org chart for a company of one is theatre. Worse, it fragments the app: six lists that don't know about each other, so a won deal doesn't reserve capacity and a late invoice doesn't flag a client at risk. Model the loop instead. Your three parts are simply which segment of it you're standing in.

Leadership — sales & growth Working — strategy, production, delivery Operations — client success & finance ↺ stage 11 feeds stage 01 — the referral loop closes here

The join that makes it work

Stage 04 must read the capacity ledger before you can send a proposal, and stage 11 must write back into stage 01 as a referral source. Those two joins are the difference between an app that reports on your business and an app that runs it. Neither is possible in a three-department layout.

Where the outline breaks

Six gaps, in the order they'll hurt you

These are not features to add later. Each one is a place where your current structure silently permits a failure that a one-person agency cannot absorb.

01 Missing object · highest severity

There is no capacity ledger, so nothing stops you selling time you don't have

Your model can happily hold four signed retainers and a full production queue and never once compare them to the hours in the month. Utilization benchmarks of 65–80% assume a person who only delivers. You also sell, quote, chase invoices, write reports and do your own admin — so realistic billable capacity for a solo operator is closer to 40–55% of working hours, and above 85% you have no absorption left for a sick day.

Fix A first-class CapacityBlock object: hours available per week, split into billable / sales / admin / growth. Every Engagement reserves against it. The proposal screen refuses to generate a quote that pushes the month over your ceiling — or forces you to name what gets moved.
02 Naming collision · blocks all reporting

"Incumbents, Leads, Reach, Final" are three different kinds of thing wearing one coat

A monthly revenue target is a number. A list of companies you want to work with is a set of records. "Has interacted with us" is a stage on a record. Fusing them means you can never compute a conversion rate, because you can't tell what the denominator is.

Fix Split into Target (a period goal: revenue, new clients, qualified leads), Account (a company, with a source and a fit score) and Stage on the Opportunity: New → Contacted → Engaged → Qualified → Proposed → Won / Lost, with a lost-reason. Then stage-to-stage conversion falls out for free, and you can compare it to the benchmark set — roughly 15% MQL→SQL and 6–9% closed-won across B2B.
03 Missing feedback loop · compounds monthly

Nothing records what a job was supposed to cost versus what it actually cost

This is the loop that makes every future quote better, and it's absent. Without logged actuals you're estimating from the inside view, which is systematically optimistic — and with 52% of projects taking uncontrolled scope change, the gap is not small. On a fixed fee it comes straight out of your margin, invisibly.

Fix Every Deliverable carries estimated_hours and accumulates logged_hours from Sessions. Variance is shown live, and at quote time the app offers the actual distribution from your last N similar deliverables — reference-class forecasting on your own history. Also log every out-of-scope request as a ScopeChange: approved, absorbed, or declined. "Absorbed" is your real discount rate.
04 Wrong metric family · finance

"Made, spent, remaining" can't see the two things that actually end solo agencies

Those three numbers are a snapshot of profit. Businesses die of timing and concentration. The median small business runs on 27 days of cash and a quarter on 13 or fewer, so a single 45-day-late invoice is an existential event, not an annoyance. And one client at 40% of revenue means their budget freeze is your budget freeze.

Fix Add four numbers to the money view: cash buffer days (cash ÷ average daily outflow), AR aging (per invoice: sent → due → paid, with days overdue), revenue concentration (largest client as % of trailing 3-month revenue) and effective hourly rate (fee ÷ logged hours, per engagement). Agencies target no client above 10–12%; solo that's unreachable, so set your own trigger — at 35% the app tells you to go find one more client.
05 Lagging-only signals · client success

A monthly report tells you a client left; it doesn't warn you they're leaving

Client success as you've scoped it is a reporting function. Retention is worth defending — the classic finding is a 25–85% profit lift from a 5-point defection cut, though that's a ceiling and scales with margin — but you can only defend it with leading signals. And don't reach for NPS: it explains around 1% of variance in share of wallet and predicts worse than plain satisfaction.

Fix A computed health score per client from behaviour you already have: days since last approval, reply latency trend, revision rounds vs. their baseline, invoice payment lateness, scope-change friction, days since you last showed them a result. Three ambers = a flag on the weekly review, with a named recovery action.
06 Unbounded work · the "getting lost" problem

Nothing in the model can ever say "no, not yet"

You described the fear precisely — missing things, drowning in volume — but every container in your outline is an unbounded list. Little's Law is the direct answer: lead time is WIP divided by throughput, so with a fixed throughput of one person, the only lever you have is holding fewer things open. Combined with a ~23-minute recovery cost per context switch, parallel work is the thing quietly eating your week.

Fix Hard WIP limits, enforced in the UI, not suggested: max 3–5 active engagements, max 2 deliverables in Produce at once, max 1 growth experiment per quarter. Exceeding one requires explicitly parking something else. Plus a single Capture inbox — one keystroke, no fields, triaged in the weekly review — so nothing needs holding in your head between reviews.
The structure to build

Objects are what exists. Rituals are when you touch them. Signals are what the app says back. Views are the only four screens you actually need. Your Leadership / Working / Operations split lives in the Signals layer, as report groupings — exactly where it earns its keep.

Your website-as-process idea

Make it a playbook, not an embed

This was one of your better instincts and it deserves a sharper form. Checklists work in aviation and surgery because complexity has outgrown memory — the failure mode isn't ignorance, it's skipping a step you know. That's exactly a solo operator running six clients through the same process.

Structure each service on your site as a Playbook: an ordered list of steps, each with inputs, output, an owner (you or the client) and an optional gate that blocks progress until satisfied. Version it. When you win a deal, the app creates a PlaybookRun — a live copy pinned to version N, so improving the template never disturbs work in flight.

Three gates are worth enforcing from day one, because each maps to a delay you're already having:

GateBlocksWhy it exists
Access receivedProduction startMissing logins and asset access is the most common kickoff delay in solo delivery. Make it a blocking checklist item, not a polite reminder.
Brief approvedCreative productionProducing against an unapproved brief is how one round of revisions becomes four. The approval timestamp is also your scope-change baseline.
Definition of doneMarking deliveredWritten per deliverable type at template level: formats, dimensions, captions, filenames. Stops the "one more small thing" tail.

Scope discipline

What not to build

You're one person building a tool for one person while also running the agency that pays for it. Every one of these is a month you don't have, replacing something you can already buy.

✕

Invoicing and accounting. Keep issuing invoices where you do now. Record a MoneyEvent with the amount, due date and paid date — that's all the signals need.

✕

File storage and asset hosting. Store links. Your drive already does versioning, sharing and backup better than you will.

✕

A client portal. Zero clients have asked. Build the monthly report generator instead — same value, a tenth of the work, and it forces the review to happen.

✕

Email and messaging integration. A "next action + date" field on the opportunity does 90% of what a CRM inbox does, at 2% of the build.

✕

Credential storage. Track whether access was granted, never the secret itself. Keep secrets in a password manager.

✕

Multi-user roles and permissions. Add them the week you hire someone, not before. It's a rewrite either way.

✕

An NPS survey module. It explains around 1% of variance in share of wallet. The behavioural health score is free and predicts better.
